Core Foundations

What Do These Terms Mean?

Clear, jargon-free explanations of the core legal, regulatory, and fiduciary disciplines governing modern corporate boards.

Governance

Corporate Governance

"The rules and processes that ensure a company is run fairly and legally."

The system of rules, practices, and fiduciary protocols by which a firm is directed, administered, and controlled. It defines the distribution of rights and responsibilities among board directors, management, shareholders, and auditors.

Key Mandate: Prevents unilateral executive mismanagement and satisfies stock exchange listing standards (NYSE/Nasdaq).
Enforcement

Regulatory Compliance

"Strict adherence to federal disclosure laws and administrative regulations."

The ongoing process of aligning enterprise operations with statutory laws enforced by the SEC, DOJ, CFTC, and international bodies. This encompasses SEC Regulations S-K, S-X, Form 8-K timelines, and FCPA anti-bribery standards.

Key Mandate: Shields the corporation and individual directors from federal subpoenas, fines, and criminal prosecution.
Risk Controls

Enterprise Risk (ERM)

"Catching operational and financial threats before they trigger catastrophe."

A structured, board-level methodology aligned with COSO 2013 and PCAOB standards. It equips Audit Committees to detect, assess, and mitigate internal control deficiencies over financial reporting (SOX 404) and emergent technological threats.

Key Mandate: Eliminates restatements and prevents external auditor qualification of financial statements.
Director Oath

Fiduciary Duty & Caremark

"The director's non-negotiable legal obligation to protect shareholder value."

Under Delaware General Corporation Law § 141, directors owe two fundamental duties: Duty of Care (acting on an informed basis after reasonable inquiry) and Duty of Loyalty (acting solely in company interest without undisclosed personal benefit).

Key Mandate: Upholds the Business Judgment Rule in Delaware Chancery Court to defeat shareholder derivative suits.
